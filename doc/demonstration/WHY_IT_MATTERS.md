# Why this matters

The physical task tracker is not a productivity app. It serves the people who
grow a third of the world's food, work its most dangerous jobs, and lose
double-digit shares of what they produce after harvest — with paper records
and memory as their only tracking tools.

## Who it serves

- **475 million smallholder farms** (under 2 ha), producing roughly a third of
  global food (FAO: 32%; Ricciardi et al.: 28–31% of crops) — up to 60% of
  national production in low and lower-middle income countries, and ~80% of
  food in Africa and Asia. 2.5 billion people depend on family farms. These
  farms get only a small fraction of climate finance.
  ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1666054/),
  [ActionAid 2026](https://actionaid.org/publications/2026/transforming-food-systems-ground))
- The same pattern holds in construction: fragmented crews, paper punch lists,
  continuity lost between visits and handoffs.

## What goes wrong without tracking

- **Post-harvest loss:** ~40% of grain across Africa (> $4B/yr; $720M in Kenya
  alone), 30–40% in India, 30% in Eswatini, 22% of produce in Jordan. In
  Odisha strawberries, losses ran 8% on well-managed farms vs 31% on poor
  ones — management quality, i.e. *tracked* work, is the difference. A Jiangsu
  digitization pilot cut loss 46%, delays 40%, logistics cost 33%.
- **Safety:** agriculture is the deadliest industry in the UK and Australia;
  US farm occupations ran 19.4 deaths/100k in 2024 (11th most dangerous);
  Michigan farmworkers die at 12× the all-worker rate. Top causes — vehicles,
  grain bins, falls — are exactly the hazards a vision layer can flag from
  work photos.
- **Continuity:** seasons span months; workers rotate; contractors hand off.
  State lives in heads and camera rolls. Every handoff re-learns the project.

## Why now

Open-vocabulary segmentation (SAM), phone-depth geometry, and reasoning
models that read photos finally make automatic state-tracking possible without
BIM models, fixed cameras, or enterprise contracts — the three things small
farms and small contractors will never have.
